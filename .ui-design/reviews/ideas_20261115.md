# Round 2: 20 more ideas (motion, design, navigation)

**Date:** 2026-11-15 · **Branch tested:** `ux/audit-20-fixes`
**Tested:** desktop and 375px phone, light and dark, keyboard and search.

All motion should sit behind `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, use 150–250ms with `ease-out` for entering and `ease-in` for leaving, and animate only `transform` and `opacity`.

## Fixed during this round
- **Search ignored companies with no cases** (IBM, Apple, Meta and 107 others). It now lists them as "No published cases"; picking one explains that a case appears only once it meets the evidence standard. A search with no match at all says "Nothing in the tracker matches '…'" and links to the methodology, instead of offering a search that returns nothing. "Search all records" now shows a match count.
- **A direct `#about` link scrolled past the page top**, because the article's `id="about"` matched the URL fragment. Renamed to `aboutDoc`.

## A. Motion (7)

1. **Drawer slides in, backdrop fades.** It currently pops in. Slide the drawer `translateX(24px) → 0` with opacity over 220ms, fade the backdrop in 180ms, and reverse faster (150ms) on close. Prev/Next slides the content 12px left or right in the direction of travel, so stepping through cases feels like paging.
2. **Bars grow and numbers count up on first load.** The per-year bars rise from 0 (staggered 15ms per bar, 400ms total) and the three headline stats count up from 0 over 600ms. This only plays on first load, not on every filter change.
3. **Filter changes animate instead of snapping.** When a filter changes, the timeline rows re-sort with a FLIP animation (each row slides to its new position over 200ms). Dots that drop out fade, and new ones scale in from 0.6. People then see *what* changed, not just that something did.
4. **Hover card on timeline dots (desktop).** Replace the slow native `title` tooltip with a small card that fades and rises 4px in 120ms: name, role, month, tier and the first line of the summary. Keyboard focus shows the same card.
5. **Sticky header gains depth on scroll.** When the page scrolls past 8px, the header (and the sticky year row) gets a soft bottom shadow and slightly blurred background (`backdrop-filter: blur(8px)` on a translucent `--bg`). That signals "content is passing under here".
6. **View switch cross-fade.** Use the View Transitions API (`document.startViewTransition`) around `route()` for tab changes. It's a 150ms cross-fade, plus a shared-element morph of the H1. Browsers without the API simply skip it.
7. **Small feedback moments.** The search suggestions fade and scale from 0.98; a chip removed from the filter summary shrinks out; the "Link copied" toast slides up 8px; the calendar's selected cell pulses once; the Latest card lifts 2px on hover.

## B. Visual design (7)

8. **Evidence ladder instead of a plain pill.** Show the evidence level as a 6-step meter (●●●●○○ style, strongest left), with the current tier highlighted, in the drawer, Records and the Latest cards. Readers grasp "how solid is this" at a glance, and it teaches the tier order without a trip to About.
9. **Richer drawer header.** Add a band at the top with the company name as a large mono "ticker-style" label, a scope tag (Fortune 500 / Global 500 / Other notable) and sector. Under the facts, add a mini "case timeline": became public → outcome date, when the record has them.
10. **Outcome bar under the headline stats.** Add a thin stacked bar showing Lost their role vs Kept their role (and Relationship vs Misconduct). It turns the stats from three numbers into a picture, and each segment is a filter shortcut.
11. **Year columns you can click.** Clicking a bar in "Per year" filters to that year: the Latest strip and stats update, and a "Year: 2018 ×" chip appears. Hovering a bar highlights its column through all rows.
12. **Calendar redesign as a year strip.** On phones the 12-column heat map is cramped (22px cells, 9px numbers). Offer a vertical "year strip" layout there: one row per year with proportional ticks per case, tapping a year expands its months.
13. **Distinct misconduct colour.** Misconduct is drawn in `--ink-2` (near-black, or light grey in dark mode), so it reads as "inactive" next to the red relationship dots. Give it its own token (a deep plum or slate), and check both colours for 3:1 contrast in light and dark.
14. **Brand and share polish.** Add a favicon (the red dot), `theme-color` meta for both themes, Open Graph/Twitter cards, and a per-case share image generated at build time, so shared case links show a clean card with name, company and tier.

## C. Navigation and features (6)

15. **Company pages.** `#company/<id>` would show the company's name, scope, sector and stats, a mini timeline of its cases, and the cases as cards. Clicking a company goes there instead of to a filtered Records table, and companies with no cases get an honest "No published cases" page.
16. **"New since your last visit".** Remember the newest `disclosed`/`id` seen in `localStorage` (try/catch, per browser only). On return, show a "3 new cases since your last visit" pill above Latest, and mark those cards and dots with a small "New" badge.
17. **Breakdown view.** A fifth tab, or a section on Timeline, with simple ranked bars by sector, country/scope, category and outcome. Each bar is a link that applies that filter. It answers "which industries?" without anyone needing to read 147 rows.
18. **Keyboard shortcuts and a help sheet.** `?` opens a small sheet listing the shortcuts: `/` search, `g t / g c / g r / g a` switch views, `j/k` or `←/→` Prev/Next in the drawer, `Esc` close. Show the sheet once as a hint on desktop.
19. **Theme toggle.** Add a small sun/moon button in the footer or header that sets `data-theme` on `<html>` (the CSS already supports `:root[data-theme]`). Remember the choice in `localStorage`, defaulting to the system setting.
20. **Print and save-as-PDF view of a case.** Add a print stylesheet that drops the chrome and prints the drawer content as a clean one-page brief: name, company, tier, summary, facts and full source URLs. Researchers and journalists can then save or cite a case.
